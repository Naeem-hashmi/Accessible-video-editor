ClipScribe user guide

ClipScribe is a video editor for Windows designed first for screen reader users. It works with NVDA, JAWS and Narrator, and everything is done from the keyboard. Each section of this guide is a heading, so you can press H in your screen reader's browse mode to jump between them. The full list of keys is in the keyboard reference.

The idea in one minute

A video is made of layers, stacked like sheets of paper. Layer 1 is at the bottom. Video layers hold video clips, pictures and colour cards; a higher video layer appears in front of a lower one, which is how you put a logo or a small camera picture over the main video. Audio layers hold music, voice and sound effects; all layers' sound is mixed together.

Each layer holds items that play one after another, from left to right in time. There can be a gap before an item, and a transition between two touching items.

The playhead is the current time. Playing starts from it, splitting happens at it, and markers are added at it.

The window

The window has five areas, like Microsoft Clipchamp. Press F6 to move to the next area and Shift F6 to go back, or jump straight to one:

  • Control 1, Library (left): Import, Export and Save buttons, then tabs. Media lists the files in your project; Sounds has the built-in and your own sounds; Free music online searches for music you may use; Text has ready-made text styles; Templates holds built-in and saved templates. Control Tab moves between tabs.
  • Control 2, Timeline (middle): the items of the focused layer, in time order. This is where you spend most of your time. Each item is read as, for example, "2 of 5, interview.mp4, video, starts 12.5 seconds, length 30 seconds".
  • Control 3, Properties (right): quick settings for the focused item: name or text, length, fades, volume, mute, position, size, opacity, and font and animation for text. Change a field and press Enter (Control Enter in the text box) to apply.
  • Control 4, Preview (middle top): the picture. When it has focus it describes what is on screen at the playhead. Playback and timeline keys still work here.
  • Control 5, Layers: every layer, for example "Layer 3, Audio, Music, 2 items, muted".

Along the top is a toolbar of icon buttons: Import, Add text, Sounds, Play or pause, Stop, Split, Delete item, Add marker, Position, Describe picture, Undo, Redo, Templates, Save, Export and Keys. Tab moves into it; Left and Right arrow move between its buttons; Space or Enter presses one; Tab moves on to the library. Each button says its name and its shortcut.

You never have to use shortcuts: Tab and Shift Tab move between the toolbar, the library, the preview, the layers, the timeline and the properties, and the arrow keys move inside each list. The shortcuts are there to make it faster.

Laptops without a number pad: every command has a key that works on a laptop. Where a command uses Home, End, Page Up or Page Down, there is also a Control or Shift arrow alternative, and position mode uses the number row (1 to 9) as well as the number pad. Press Control H for the full list.

The menu bar (press Alt) has every command and shows its key. The status bar shows the last message, the playhead time and the listening mode; your screen reader's status bar command reads it (NVDA+End, or Insert+Page Down in JAWS).

Moving around: a grid of layers and time

  • Left arrow and Right arrow: previous and next item on this layer. The playhead moves to the start of the item. Home and End go to the first and last item.
  • Up arrow and Down arrow: the layer above or below in the list, landing on the item at the same time. This works with any number of layers, so 9, 20 or 50 layers are all easy to reach.
  • Alt 1 to Alt 9: jump straight to layers 1 to 9, and listen to that layer on its own.
  • Alt 0: listen to all layers together. Press again to go back to listening to the focused layer only.
  • Control L: type a layer number to jump to it, for layer 10 and above.
  • Control G: type a time to move the playhead, such as 1:30, 90, 1m 30s, or +5 to move 5 seconds on.
  • Comma and period move the playhead 1 second; with Shift 10 seconds; with Control one frame. Control Home and Control End go to the start and end.

Playing and listening

  • K or Space: play from the playhead, or pause. When you pause, the program says where you stopped.
  • L: play; press again for double, then four times speed, then normal again.
  • J: jump back 5 seconds (you can change this in Preferences).
  • Escape: stop.
  • T: say the playhead time and how far into the current item it is. W: say what every layer has at the playhead, including where pictures are placed. Shift T: a summary of the whole project.

Playback starts at once: the program mixes the project live from the playhead, so there is nothing to prepare and no file to wait for. It plays through the normal Windows sound output and does not need Windows Media Player or the Media Feature Pack. You can keep editing while it plays: pressing S while playing splits exactly where you are listening.

Mute and solo

  • M: mute or unmute the focused layer. Muted layers are not heard and are left out of the export.
  • Shift M: solo the focused layer, which mutes all the others. Press again to unmute them.
  • Control Shift M: unmute every layer.

Adding videos, pictures and sound

  • Control I: choose one or more files to add after the last item of the focused layer. Sound files go to an audio layer automatically, and pictures and videos to a video layer.
  • Control Shift I: add files at the playhead without moving anything. The program finds a layer with free space at that time, or makes a new one. Use this for a logo that appears at a certain moment.
  • Control Shift E: the sound library. First come real recordings, the sounds you hear in reels and shorts: keyboard typing, typewriter, mouse and button clicks, whooshes and swipes, pops, camera shutter, notification dings, cash register, applause, laughter, drum roll, rimshot, cymbal, bells, water drops, footsteps, a door knock, a heartbeat, a ticking clock, rain, thunder, wind, birds and more. They were recorded by people and shared on Freesound as public domain (CC0), so they are free in any video with no credit needed. After them come the effects the program makes itself, and your own sounds from Documents, ClipScribe, Sound Effects. Press Space to listen and Enter to add at the playhead. The same list is in the library's Sounds tab.
  • Control Shift C: a solid colour card, for a title background or a pause.
  • Control Shift V and Control Shift A: a new video or audio layer.

Cutting and trimming

  • S: split the item at the playhead into two.
  • Shift left bracket: trim away the part of the item before the playhead. Shift right bracket: trim away the part after it.
  • Delete: delete the focused item. Control Delete: delete it but leave the time empty, so later items do not move.
  • Control Z undoes anything, and says what it undid. Control Y redoes.

Ripple

Ripple is on at first. With ripple on, deleting, trimming or sliding an item moves the later items on the same layer too, so no holes appear. With ripple off, later items keep their time, which is what you want when music or a logo must stay exactly in place. Control R switches ripple on and off.

Range and markers

  • Left bracket sets the range start at the playhead, and right bracket sets the range end.
  • Shift Delete removes everything between range start and range end on the focused layer. Export can also export only the range.
  • B adds a named marker at the playhead.
  • Alt left arrow and Alt right arrow jump the playhead to the previous and next marker. The range start and end count as markers too.
  • Shift B lists every marker. From the list you can go to a marker, rename or move it, delete it, split the item there, or add a transition there.

Arranging items

  • Alt Shift left arrow and Alt Shift right arrow: swap the item with the one before or after it, changing the order. For example, to put item 3 first, go to it and press Alt Shift Home.
  • Alt J and Alt L: slide the item earlier or later in time by 1 second. With Shift added, by a tenth of a second.
  • Control Shift J and Control Shift L: slide the whole layer.
  • Alt Shift up arrow and Alt Shift down arrow: move the focused layer up or down the list. For video layers, a higher number is in front.
  • Control C, Control X, Control V and Control D copy, cut, paste and duplicate items.

Text: titles, captions and credits

  • Control Shift T adds text at the playhead. Type the words (Enter starts a new line, Tab moves on) and choose a style: Title, Lower third, Caption, Top headline, Typewriter, Credits or Call to action. The other tabs set the font, colour, size, box, position and animation.
  • Text goes on its own "Text" layer above your video, so it never replaces a clip.
  • Ways in and out: appear, fade, slide from the left, right, top or bottom, float up gently, and typewriter (letter by letter).
  • F2 changes the words. Enter opens all options, including a Text tab.
  • The library's Text tab lists the same styles; Enter on one adds text in that style.

Where things are on screen

Press Shift W to hear the picture at the playhead, from the bottom layer up. For example: "Layer 1, interview.mp4, full frame. Layer 2, logo.png, top right, 11 percent wide and 20 percent high, over interview.mp4. Layer 3, text "Welcome", bottom centre, 52 percent wide, over interview.mp4." The frame is thought of as a 3 by 3 grid: top left, top centre, top right, middle left, centre, middle right, bottom left, bottom centre and bottom right. You also hear which edges a picture touches, whether it is cropped or see-through, what it covers, and whether parts of the frame are black.

Position mode: moving, resizing and cropping with the keyboard

On a picture, video or text, press P. This is the keyboard version of dragging on the screen. Until you press Enter or Escape:

  • Arrows move it 5 percent of the frame; with Shift, 1 percent. You hear the new position, for example "65 percent across, 10 percent down, touching the right edge".
  • Plus and minus make it bigger and smaller.
  • 1 to 9 snap it to a place laid out like a number pad: 7 top left, 8 top centre, 9 top right, 4 middle left, 5 centre, 6 middle right, 1 bottom left, 2 bottom centre, 3 bottom right.
  • 0 makes it full frame (maximise). F then switches between fit (the whole picture, maybe with black bars) and fill (covers the frame, cutting off edges). Use minus or a number key to make it small again (minimise).
  • Control with an arrow crops 5 percent from that edge; Control Shift with an arrow gives it back; R removes all cropping.
  • O and Shift O make it more or less see-through.
  • D describes it and the whole picture. F1 repeats these keys.
  • Enter keeps the changes; Escape puts everything back as it was.

After every move a short tone plays. It comes from the left or right speaker depending on how far across the picture is, and is higher the nearer the picture is to the top. With headphones you can hear the position as well as be told it.

Sound of each item and layer

  • Control Up and Control Down: the focused item louder or quieter by 10 percent (up to 400 percent).
  • Control M: mute or unmute only this item's sound.
  • Control Shift Up and Control Shift Down: the whole layer louder or quieter.
  • In properties (Control 3) and item options (Enter, Sound tab): volume, mute, fade in and out, noise reduction, hum removal, rumble removal, a low, middle and high equaliser, voice presets and loudness levelling.

Free music and sounds online

Control Shift O opens the library's Free music online tab. Type what you want, for example "calm piano" or "rain", choose Music or Sound effects, and press Enter. Each result says its title, artist, length and licence. Space previews; Enter downloads it and adds it at the playhead.

Only two kinds of licence are shown, both safe for monetised videos and for trimming: public domain (CC0, no credit needed) and CC BY (credit needed). For CC BY sounds the program saves the credit with the project, and when you export it writes a file named after your video, ending in "credits.txt". Copy its lines into your video description. F7 also reminds you. The search uses Openverse and needs an internet connection; about 20 searches a minute are allowed.

Templates

  • Control Shift N saves the project as a template. Give it a name and a folder (your own folders, such as "Vlogs" or "Tutorials"), and choose whether to keep the media files. Layers, their settings, text and colour cards are always kept, so a template can hold a ready-made intro title.
  • Control Shift K opens the Templates tab: Enter starts a new project, Delete removes a template, and buttons rename, move to another folder, import a template file from someone else, or open the templates folder (Documents, ClipScribe, Templates).
  • Control N lists every template, built-in and yours; type to search.

Item options

Press Enter on an item. The options dialog has tabs; press Control Tab to move between them.

  • General: name, where in the file to start, length, gap before, fade in and fade out.
  • Transition in: the transition from the previous item into this one. Picture transitions include cross fade, fade through black or white, dissolve, wipes and slides in four directions, cover and reveal, circle and box shapes, clock wipe, barn doors, pixelate, blur and zoom. Sound crossfades come in many curves, from linear to smooth (good for music) and exponential. A transition only plays when the two items touch.
  • Picture, on video layers: position (full frame, centre, the four corners, top, bottom and sides, or custom), size, opacity, fill, and cropping from each edge.
  • Text, for text items: the words, font, size, colour, bold, outline, box, and how it comes in and goes out.
  • Sound: volume up to 400 percent, mute, noise reduction (light, medium or strong), hum removal (50 or 60 hertz), rumble removal, a three-band equaliser (low at 100 hertz, middle at 1000 hertz, high at 8000 hertz, each from minus 12 to plus 12 decibels), voice presets (clear, warm, broadcast) and loudness levelling.

Control T chooses the transition at the join nearest the playhead without opening the whole dialog. D reads every setting of the focused item.

Layer options and templates

Alt Enter opens the layer options: name, layer volume, and defaults for new items, such as how long pictures show, the default transition, and the default position for a logo layer.

Control N starts a new project from a template: basic video, YouTube video with music and logo, photo slideshow, picture in picture, podcast to video, or vertical short. Templates only set up layers and defaults; they contain no media of anyone else's.

Checking and exporting

  • F7 checks the project for things a sighted editor would notice: black gaps, missing files, transitions that cannot play, muted layers, or picture ending before the sound. Press Enter on a finding to go there.
  • Control E exports. Choose a format: YouTube 1080p (recommended), 720p, 4K, vertical for Shorts and Reels, square, the project size, or audio only as MP3, WAV or M4A. Progress is spoken every 10 percent; Escape cancels.
  • Control S saves the project. A recovery copy is also kept automatically, and offered if the program ever closes unexpectedly.

Speech and sounds

When NVDA or JAWS is running, the program talks to it directly; otherwise it uses Windows UI Automation, which Narrator also reads. Short sounds confirm actions without words: moving, reaching an edge, changing layer, adding a marker, an error, muting. Choose a sound theme (Soft, Classic, Crisp, Retro or Off) and its volume in Preferences (Control Shift comma). Shift V switches between brief, normal and detailed descriptions.

To make your own sound theme, use Tools, "Copy the current sound theme to my themes folder", replace the WAV files with your own (keep the file names), and choose your theme in Preferences.

Changing keys

Tools, "Open the keyboard settings file" opens a text file listing every command and its keys. Change the keys, save, and restart the program. If your keyboard layout has no bracket keys, this is how to move those commands to other keys.

Getting help

F1 lists every command with its keys; type to search, then press Enter to run one. Help, Getting started gives a short summary. If something goes wrong, Tools, "Open the log file" shows details you can send to the developers.
