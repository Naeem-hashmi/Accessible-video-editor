ClipScribe user guide

ClipScribe is a video editor for Windows designed first for screen reader users. It works with NVDA, JAWS and Narrator, and everything is done from the keyboard. Each section of this guide is a heading, so you can press H in your screen reader's browse mode to jump between them. The full list of keys is in the keyboard reference.

The idea in one minute

A video is made of layers, stacked like sheets of paper. Layer 1 is at the bottom. Video layers hold video clips, pictures and colour cards; a higher video layer appears in front of a lower one, which is how you put a logo or a small camera picture over the main video. Audio layers hold music, voice and sound effects; all layers' sound is mixed together.

Each layer holds items that play one after another, from left to right in time. There can be a gap before an item, and a transition between two touching items.

The playhead is the current time. Playing starts from it, splitting happens at it, and markers are added at it.

The quickest way: Quick video in three steps

Press Control Shift Q (or the Quick video button on the Home tab).

  1. Choose your files: videos and pictures, in a normal Open dialog. Several at once is fine.
  2. Choose a style: Reel (vertical for Reels, Shorts and TikTok, quick cuts on the beat with a whoosh), YouTube (landscape, short crossfades, title and thank-you card), Slideshow (photos change on the beat with slow crossfades) or Talking video (long pauses shortened, music low under the voice). Choose music: none, a built-in music bed, or your own file.
  3. Give it a title and choose whether to add captions, then press OK.

ClipScribe then, in this order: puts the files in name order (so "clip 2" comes before "clip 10"); fills the frame with each picture; trims silence at the start and end of speech clips (and shortens long pauses for Reels and talking videos); starts the music on its first beat and repeats it to the end with a fade-out; makes pictures change on the beat; adds the style's transitions, the title and, for YouTube, an end card; lowers the music wherever someone speaks; adds captions if you asked; and levels the whole video to -14 LUFS, the loudness YouTube, Instagram and TikTok play at. It tells you what it did, for example "Made a 48 second reel from 9 files, with cut to the beat at 120 beats per minute, a whoosh on every cut, captions". The result is a normal project: every part can be changed afterwards.

The window

The window is laid out like Word or Visual Studio Code. Press F6 to move to the next area and Shift F6 to go back. The order follows the screen:

  • Ribbon (top): tabs of icon buttons. Home (undo, cut, copy, paste, split, delete, Quick video, import, save, export), Insert (files, text, sounds, free music, colour card, layers, marker), Transitions (choose the transition into the selected item), Animations (how text comes in and goes out, fades), Audio (volume, mute, solo, lower music under speech, shorten pauses, find beats, cut to the beat), Captions (transcribe, edit by transcript, make captions), Picture (position mode, describe) and Export. Left and Right arrow change tab; Tab goes into the tab's buttons; arrows move between them. Every button says its name and its key.
  • Control 1, Library (left): Import, Export and Save buttons, then tabs with icons down the left side, like the activity bar in VS Code. Media lists the files in your project; Sounds has real recordings, built-in effects and your own sounds; Free music online searches for music you may use; Text has ready-made text styles; Templates holds built-in and saved templates. Up and Down arrow move between the tabs. In sound lists, Space plays a sound and Enter adds it.
  • Control 4, Preview (centre): the picture, large. When it has focus it describes what is on screen at the playhead. Under it are the play controls: go to start, rewind, play or pause, fast forward, stop, previous and next marker, and listen to all layers, with the time.
  • Control 5, Layers (bottom left): every layer, for example "Layer 3, Audio, Music, 2 items, muted". Up and Down arrow choose a layer; Left and Right arrow go through that layer's items, saying each one, while you stay on the layer. Enter goes into the items.
  • Control 2, Timeline (bottom): the items of the focused layer, in time order. Each item is read as, for example, "2 of 5, interview.mp4, video, starts 12.5 seconds, length 30 seconds". Below the list, a drawing of all layers along time is there for the mouse: click an item to select it, click empty space to move the playhead, drag an item to move it, drag its right end to change its length, double-click for its options, right-click for a menu, and Control with the mouse wheel to zoom.
  • Control 3, Inspector (right): options for whatever is selected. When the Layers list has focus it shows the layer: name, volume, mute, and smart tools for it. When an item is selected it shows that item's options, in sections: for text, the words, font, size, bold, colour, outline, background, how it comes in and goes out; for pictures and video, position, size, fill the frame, margin from the edge, rounded corners, opacity and crop; for sound, volume, mute, noise reduction, voice preset and even loudness; and the transition from the item before. Change a field and press Enter (Control Enter in the text box) to apply.

You never have to use shortcuts: Tab and Shift Tab move between the areas and the arrow keys move inside each one. The mouse works everywhere too. The shortcuts are there to make it faster.

Laptops without a number pad: every command has a key that works on a laptop. Where a command uses Home, End, Page Up or Page Down, there is also a Control or Shift arrow alternative, and position mode uses the number row (1 to 9) as well as the number pad. Press Control H for the full list.

The menu bar (press Alt) has every command and shows its key, including the Smart tools menu. The status bar shows the last message, the playhead time and the listening mode; your screen reader's status bar command reads it (NVDA+End, or Insert+Page Down in JAWS).

Moving around: a grid of layers and time

  • Left arrow and Right arrow: previous and next item on this layer. The playhead moves to the start of the item. Home and End go to the first and last item.
  • Up arrow and Down arrow: the layer above or below in the list, landing on the item at the same time. This works with any number of layers, so 9, 20 or 50 layers are all easy to reach.
  • Alt 1 to Alt 9: jump straight to layers 1 to 9, and listen to that layer on its own.
  • Alt 0: listen to all layers together. Press again to go back to listening to the focused layer only.
  • Control L: type a layer number to jump to it, for layer 10 and above.
  • Control G: type a time to move the playhead, such as 1:30, 90, 1m 30s, or +5 to move 5 seconds on.
  • Comma and period move the playhead 1 second; with Shift 10 seconds; with Control one frame. Control Home and Control End go to the start and end.

Playing and listening

  • K or Space: play from the playhead, or pause. When you pause, the program says where you stopped.
  • L: play; press again for double, then four times speed, then normal again.
  • J: jump back 5 seconds (you can change this in Preferences).
  • Escape: stop.
  • T: say the playhead time and how far into the current item it is. W: say what every layer has at the playhead, including where pictures are placed. Shift T: a summary of the whole project.

Playback starts at once: the program mixes the project live from the playhead, so there is nothing to prepare and no file to wait for. It plays through the normal Windows sound output and does not need Windows Media Player or the Media Feature Pack. You can keep editing while it plays: pressing S while playing splits exactly where you are listening.

Mute and solo

  • M: mute or unmute the focused layer. Muted layers are not heard and are left out of the export.
  • Shift M: solo the focused layer, which mutes all the others. Press again to unmute them.
  • Control Shift M: unmute every layer.

Adding videos, pictures and sound

  • Control I: choose one or more files to add after the last item of the focused layer. Sound files go to an audio layer automatically, and pictures and videos to a video layer.
  • Control Shift I: add files at the playhead without moving anything. The program finds a layer with free space at that time, or makes a new one. Use this for a logo that appears at a certain moment.
  • Control Shift E: the sound library. First come real recordings made by people, the sounds you hear in reels and shorts: keyboard typing, a typewriter bell, mouse clicks, a bicycle bell, wind chimes, a cash register, coins, applause, cheering, laughter, an audience going "ooh", a drum roll, a thud, door knocks, a ticking clock, a page turning, pouring water, rain and thunder. They come from Wikimedia Commons and are public domain or CC0, so they are free in any video with no credit needed (the list of who recorded them is in the program folder, Sounds, Recordings, CREDITS.md). Whooshes, pops and swipes are among the built-in effects. After them come the effects the program makes itself, and your own sounds from Documents, ClipScribe, Sound Effects. Press Space to listen and Enter to add at the playhead. The same list is in the library's Sounds tab.
  • Control Shift C: a solid colour card, for a title background or a pause.
  • Control Shift V and Control Shift A: a new video or audio layer.

Cutting and trimming

  • S: split the item at the playhead into two.
  • Shift left bracket: trim away the part of the item before the playhead. Shift right bracket: trim away the part after it.
  • Delete: delete the focused item. Control Delete: delete it but leave the time empty, so later items do not move.
  • Control Z undoes anything, and says what it undid. Control Y redoes.

Ripple

Ripple is on at first. With ripple on, deleting, trimming or sliding an item moves the later items on the same layer too, so no holes appear. With ripple off, later items keep their time, which is what you want when music or a logo must stay exactly in place. Control R switches ripple on and off.

Range and markers

  • Left bracket sets the range start at the playhead, and right bracket sets the range end.
  • Shift Delete removes everything between range start and range end on the focused layer. Export can also export only the range.
  • B adds a named marker at the playhead.
  • Alt left arrow and Alt right arrow jump the playhead to the previous and next marker. The range start and end count as markers too.
  • Shift B lists every marker. From the list you can go to a marker, rename or move it, delete it, split the item there, or add a transition there.

Arranging items

  • Alt Shift left arrow and Alt Shift right arrow: swap the item with the one before or after it, changing the order. For example, to put item 3 first, go to it and press Alt Shift Home.
  • Alt J and Alt L: slide the item earlier or later in time by 1 second. With Shift added, by a tenth of a second. Hold the keys down and the slide speeds up: four times faster after 2 seconds, ten times after 4. However long you hold, one Control Z takes the whole slide back.
  • Control Shift J and Control Shift L: slide the whole layer.
  • Alt Shift up arrow and Alt Shift down arrow: move the focused layer up or down the list. For video layers, a higher number is in front.
  • Control C, Control X, Control V and Control D copy, cut, paste and duplicate items.

Making items longer, shorter, faster or slower

  • Alt equals and Alt minus (or the number pad plus and minus with Alt): make the item 1 second longer or shorter, keeping its start. A picture, colour card or text simply stays on longer; a video or sound clip shows more or less of its file, never past its end. Add Shift for a tenth of a second. Holding speeds up, like sliding.
  • Alt right bracket and Alt left bracket (or Control Shift equals and Control Shift minus): play a video or sound clip faster or slower, from a quarter speed (slow motion) up to 4 times. The clip keeps all its content, so faster makes it shorter and slower makes it longer. Sound keeps its normal pitch. Alt Shift right bracket or Control Shift 0 goes back to normal speed. Speed is also in the item options (Enter), and captions, the transcript, pauses, beats and music ducking all follow it.
  • With ripple off, the next item keeps its time: a longer item can only grow into the gap before it, and ClipScribe tells you when the next item is in the way.

Selecting several items at once

  • Shift right arrow and Shift left arrow select the next or previous item too, from where you started. Each step says the item and how many are selected.
  • Control Space selects or unselects just the focused item, to pick items that are not next to each other. Control A selects every item on the layer. Escape clears the selection.
  • While items are selected, these work on all of them: delete (with or without keeping the timing), copy, cut, duplicate, paste, mute, louder and quieter, sliding, longer and shorter, and faster and slower. Enter opens Settings for the selected items: volume, sound on or off, speed, one length for all, fades and the transition into each. Anything left at "Keep as is" stays as it was on every item.
  • Selected items say "Selected" in the timeline list and have an outline on the visual timeline.
  • Find beats now uses Alt B (next beat) and Alt Shift B (previous beat), since Shift with the arrows selects.

The options menu: Applications key

On an item in the timeline, press the Applications key (the menu key next to the right Control) or Shift F10, or right-click. A menu lists everything you can do with that item: All settings, rename or edit text, copy, cut, paste, duplicate, delete, split and trim, a Length and speed submenu, a Move submenu, a Sound submenu (mute, volume, transcribe, clean up words) and a Picture submenu, with each command's keys shown. When several items are selected, the menu offers the commands for all of them. On a layer in the layer list, the same key opens the layer's menu: settings, mute, solo, volume, moving, sliding everything, adding files, transcribing, captions, ducking and deleting.

Text: titles, captions and credits

  • Control Shift T adds text at the playhead. Type the words (Enter starts a new line, Tab moves on) and choose a style: Title, Lower third, Caption, Top headline, Typewriter, Credits or Call to action. The other tabs set the font, colour, size, box, position and animation.
  • Text goes on its own "Text" layer above your video, so it never replaces a clip.
  • Ways in and out: appear, fade, slide from the left, right, top or bottom, float up gently, and typewriter (letter by letter).
  • F2 changes the words. Enter opens all options, including a Text tab.
  • The library's Text tab lists the same styles; Enter on one adds text in that style.

Where things are on screen

Press Shift W to hear the picture at the playhead, from the bottom layer up. For example: "Layer 1, interview.mp4, full frame. Layer 2, logo.png, top right, 11 percent wide and 20 percent high, over interview.mp4. Layer 3, text "Welcome", bottom centre, 52 percent wide, over interview.mp4." The frame is thought of as a 3 by 3 grid: top left, top centre, top right, middle left, centre, middle right, bottom left, bottom centre and bottom right. You also hear which edges a picture touches, whether it is cropped or see-through, what it covers, and whether parts of the frame are black.

Position mode: moving, resizing and cropping with the keyboard

On a picture, video or text, press P. This is the keyboard version of dragging on the screen. Until you press Enter or Escape:

  • Arrows move it 5 percent of the frame; with Shift, 1 percent. You hear the new position, for example "65 percent across, 10 percent down, touching the right edge".
  • Plus and minus make it bigger and smaller.
  • 1 to 9 snap it to a place laid out like a number pad: 7 top left, 8 top centre, 9 top right, 4 middle left, 5 centre, 6 middle right, 1 bottom left, 2 bottom centre, 3 bottom right.
  • 0 makes it full frame (maximise). F then switches between fit (the whole picture, maybe with black bars) and fill (covers the frame, cutting off edges). Use minus or a number key to make it small again (minimise).
  • Control with an arrow crops 5 percent from that edge; Control Shift with an arrow gives it back; R removes all cropping.
  • O and Shift O make it more or less see-through.
  • D describes it and the whole picture. F1 repeats these keys.
  • Enter keeps the changes; Escape puts everything back as it was.

After every move a short tone plays. It comes from the left or right speaker depending on how far across the picture is, and is higher the nearer the picture is to the top. With headphones you can hear the position as well as be told it.

Sound of each item and layer

  • Control Up and Control Down: the focused item louder or quieter by 10 percent (up to 400 percent).
  • Control M: mute or unmute only this item's sound.
  • Control Shift Up and Control Shift Down: the whole layer louder or quieter.
  • In properties (Control 3) and item options (Enter, Sound tab): volume, mute, fade in and out, noise reduction, hum removal, rumble removal, a low, middle and high equaliser, voice presets and loudness levelling.

Free music and sounds online

Control Shift O opens the library's Free music online tab. Type what you want, for example "calm piano" or "rain", choose Music or Sound effects, and press Enter. Each result says its title, artist, length and licence. Space previews; Enter downloads it and adds it at the playhead.

Only two kinds of licence are shown, both safe for monetised videos and for trimming: public domain (CC0, no credit needed) and CC BY (credit needed). For CC BY sounds the program saves the credit with the project, and when you export it writes a file named after your video, ending in "credits.txt". Copy its lines into your video description. F7 also reminds you. The search uses Openverse and needs an internet connection; about 20 searches a minute are allowed.

Speech to text, editing by transcript and captions

ClipScribe can write down what is said in your videos, on your own computer: nothing is uploaded, and after a one-time download of the speech model it works without internet. It uses Whisper (through whisper.cpp) and understands English, Urdu, Hindi and many other languages.

  • Control Shift W, Transcribe speech: choose the language spoken (for Urdu and Hindi always choose it: automatic detection can mix the two up, because they sound alike) and a model. Accurate (574 MB) is best for Urdu, Hindi and noisy sound; Fast (148 MB) is quick and fine for clear English. Hindi is written in Devanagari and Urdu in Urdu script.
  • Control Shift X, Edit by transcript: the words open as a document, one sentence per line, in a normal text box. Read and select with your screen reader's usual keys. In Cut mode, delete words or whole lines and press Apply (or Control Enter): those parts are cut out of the video, keeping a short natural pause. Changing a word never cuts anything. In Fix text mode, correct spellings for the captions; the video stays the same. Control Space plays from the word at the cursor. Every change can be undone with Control Z.
  • Control Shift G, Make captions: short caption lines (at most 32 letters or 3.5 seconds, new line at each sentence and pause) on a Captions layer at the top, white bold text on a dark box. Urdu captions use the Noto Nastaliq Urdu font and Hindi Noto Sans Devanagari. After cutting by transcript, make captions again and they follow the new timing.

Faster transcription. By default only the parts of each file that clips actually play are transcribed (with a second to spare), and parts already done are skipped, so a 25-minute recording cut down to 4 minutes takes about 4 minutes of work. Choose "The whole files" to do everything. Silence and music are skipped before Whisper listens, which is faster and avoids made-up words. With Quick draft first ticked, the Fast model writes a draft in a fraction of the time so you can start editing at once; the model you chose then improves it in the background (the status bar shows how far it is) and replaces the draft when done, unless you corrected the draft meanwhile. In Preferences, Speech recognition, Run speed test finds the fastest number of processor threads for your computer, and the graphics chip is used when it helps. Sound that a camera recorded a little before or after its picture stays in step.

Good to know: Whisper often leaves out "um" and "uh", so to remove those use Shorten pauses, which finds them by the gaps they leave. Word times can be a tenth of a second or so off; ClipScribe moves each word's edges to where the sound really starts and stops before cutting.

Cleaning up the words: repeats, fillers and AI

  • Alt Shift R, Find repeats and fillers, on this computer: filler sounds (um, uh, hmm), a word said twice, a phrase started again ("I went to the, I went to the shop": the first try is cut and the last kept) and stray symbols. Each one is a tick box in a list, ticked to begin with: untick any you want to keep, press Enter on one to move the playhead there, then choose Cut ticked. One Control Z undoes it all.
  • Alt Shift A, Clean up with AI: sends only the words (never the video or sound) to the AI service you set up in Preferences, AI assistant, together with what the video is about. It suggests repeats, false starts, fillers and mistakes the speaker corrects, and you get the same tick list. Every suggestion is checked against the real transcript first, so a confused AI cannot cut the wrong part. Choose OpenRouter (one key for many low-cost and open-source models), Ollama (free, runs on your own computer, no key, nothing leaves the computer), OpenAI, or any other OpenAI-compatible service. Your API key is stored encrypted for your Windows account. Test the AI service checks the address, model and key.
  • Alt Shift E, Export transcript: saves the words as plain text, one sentence per line (to edit in Notepad, Word or an AI chat), or as SRT or VTT captions.
  • Alt Shift I, Import edited transcript: reads the edited file back. Words you deleted become cuts, shown in the tick list first, and words you corrected fix the transcript without cutting. Keep the sentences in their order.

Pauses, beats and music under speech

  • Control Shift H, Shorten pauses: on the selected clip, ClipScribe measures the loudness every 20 milliseconds, works out the background noise of that recording, and finds every quiet stretch. It tells you, for example, "7 pauses longer than 0.7 seconds, 12.4 seconds in all", then shortens each to 0.3 seconds (natural), 0.15 seconds (tight) or removes it. Each cut has a tiny sound fade so it never clicks.
  • Control Shift B, Find beats: on the selected music, finds the tempo and every beat ("120 beats per minute, 96 beats"). Then Alt B and Alt Shift B jump to the next and previous beat. Music with drums works best; for calm music without a beat it says so.
  • Control Shift U, Cut to the beat: every picture and clip on the selected layer ends on a beat; pictures change every 1, 2, 4 or 8 beats. Clips are never made longer than their file.
  • Control Shift D, Lower music under speech (auto-ducking): on the music layer, finds speech on the other layers and makes the music 6, 12 or 18 decibels quieter there, with smooth fades. The places show in the inspector and on the visual timeline, and it can be removed again.

Templates

  • Control Shift N saves the project as a template. Give it a name and a folder (your own folders, such as "Vlogs" or "Tutorials"), and choose whether to keep the media files. Layers, their settings, text and colour cards are always kept, so a template can hold a ready-made intro title.
  • Control Shift K opens the Templates tab: Enter starts a new project, Delete removes a template, and buttons rename, move to another folder, import a template file from someone else, or open the templates folder (Documents, ClipScribe, Templates).
  • Control N lists every template, built-in and yours; type to search.

Item options

Press Enter on an item. The options dialog has tabs; press Control Tab to move between them.

  • General: name, where in the file to start, length, gap before, fade in and fade out.
  • Transition in: the transition from the previous item into this one. Picture transitions include cross fade, fade through black or white, dissolve, wipes and slides in four directions, cover and reveal, circle and box shapes, clock wipe, barn doors, pixelate, blur and zoom. Sound crossfades come in many curves, from linear to smooth (good for music) and exponential. A transition only plays when the two items touch.
  • Picture, on video layers: position (full frame, centre, the four corners, top, bottom and sides, or custom), size, opacity, fill, and cropping from each edge.
  • Text, for text items: the words, font, size, colour, bold, outline, box, and how it comes in and goes out.
  • Sound: volume up to 400 percent, mute, noise reduction (light, medium or strong), hum removal (50 or 60 hertz), rumble removal, a three-band equaliser (low at 100 hertz, middle at 1000 hertz, high at 8000 hertz, each from minus 12 to plus 12 decibels), voice presets (clear, warm, broadcast) and loudness levelling.

Control T chooses the transition at the join nearest the playhead without opening the whole dialog. D reads every setting of the focused item.

Layer options and templates

Alt Enter opens the layer options: name, layer volume, and defaults for new items, such as how long pictures show, the default transition, and the default position for a logo layer.

Control N starts a new project from a template: basic video, YouTube video with music and logo, photo slideshow, picture in picture, podcast to video, or vertical short. Templates only set up layers and defaults; they contain no media of anyone else's.

Checking and exporting

  • F7 checks the project for things a sighted editor would notice: black gaps, missing files, transitions that cannot play, muted layers, or picture ending before the sound. Press Enter on a finding to go there.
  • Control E exports. Choose a format: YouTube 1080p (recommended), 720p, 4K, vertical for Shorts and Reels, square, the project size, or audio only as MP3, WAV or M4A. Then a speed: Fastest (the default) uses your computer's video chip (Intel Quick Sync, NVIDIA or AMD) when it really works, tested with a tiny export first, and otherwise the fastest processor setting; Balanced is about two to three times faster than Best quality with a slightly larger file. If the video chip fails part way, the export is redone on the processor by itself. The finished message says how long it took and what encoded it. Progress is spoken every 10 percent; Escape cancels.
  • Control S saves the project. A recovery copy is also kept automatically, and offered if the program ever closes unexpectedly.

Speech and sounds

The sound library (Control Shift E, or the Sounds tab of the library) has real recorded sounds for modern edits: swipes and whooshes for transitions, pops and bubbles for text and stickers, clicks and ticks, glass dings, chimes and success sounds, punches and knocks, and risers and power-ups, as well as typing, applause, laughter, rain and more. All are CC0 or public domain, free for any video, and every one starts right on its sound so it lands where you put it.

When NVDA or JAWS is running, the program talks to it directly; otherwise it uses Windows UI Automation, which Narrator also reads. Short sounds confirm actions without words: moving, reaching an edge, changing layer, adding a marker, an error, muting. Choose a sound theme (Soft, Classic, Crisp, Retro or Off) and its volume in Preferences (Control Shift comma). Shift V switches between brief, normal and detailed descriptions.

To make your own sound theme, use Tools, "Copy the current sound theme to my themes folder", replace the WAV files with your own (keep the file names), and choose your theme in Preferences.

Changing keys

Tools, "Open the keyboard settings file" opens a text file listing every command and its keys. Change the keys, save, and restart the program. If your keyboard layout has no bracket keys, this is how to move those commands to other keys.

Getting help

F1 lists every command with its keys; type to search, then press Enter to run one. Help, Getting started gives a short summary. If something goes wrong, Tools, "Open the log file" shows details you can send to the developers.
